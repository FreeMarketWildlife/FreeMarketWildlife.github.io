Strategy / Short-form content

Authenticity is King

Troy, in analyzing your Shorts, I realized that people LOVE IT when you are authentically morally upright and just. When you see bullshit, you call it out. When you see good work done well, you call that out too. When you are genuinely curious, you ask. That is what I’ll focus on when making Shorts for your channel.

I haven’t analyzed your long-form content yet. I’ll be looking at it later this week.

The finding

Give people a reason to care.

This is What Works

  • Interesting characters who are ENGAGED with you and the camera. They know they are being filmed; it never feels like a hidden camera.
  • Every one of your successful Shorts has an authority figure. Someone has knowledge or experience worth listening to.
  • Your strongest Shorts let the worker be the authority. You humble yourself, ask questions and let the person you hired share their expertise.
  • Your question creates interest. Their answer makes the video. Personality and an entertaining response make the moment worth sharing.
  • You cannot guarantee that moment in every video. Not every question gets an amazing answer from an amazing character.
  • Your most successful Shorts GIVE VALUE. Viewers leave with useful insight, a memorable perspective or something worth sharing.

Why Your Most Recent Shorts SUCK

  • Missing captions. Your dialogue needs readable captions that help viewers follow along.
  • Title captions add little value. Use that space to support the actual story.
  • Your recent Shorts feel forced. Shift from quantity to quality of STORY.
  • A long-form video may give you only one worthwhile Short. Stop forcing extra clips out of it.
  • Your recent Shorts are not optimized for retention. Build around a clear hook, tight story progression and a payoff.

The creative direction

Make trust visible.

  • Show sound judgment. Explain decisions and tradeoffs.
  • Treat people with dignity. Let them speak and take their concerns seriously.
  • Be honest about setbacks. Share mistakes and what they taught you.
  • Stay sober-minded. Be calm and thoughtful when situations get difficult.
  • Show the work. Let actions and follow-through establish credibility.
  • Share useful insight. Explain what others can learn from the process.
  • Keep it authentic. Let your character come through in real interactions.

Community comes first

Build a STRONG, healthy community that knows YOU.

  • People are watching without knowing who you are. In my analysis, commenters almost NEVER seem to know you personally, whether a video is popular or not. We need to turn that attention into a community that recognizes you.
  • Make your face your profile picture. A clear headshot helps newcomers connect your name, your face and the person in the video.
  • Pin a personal comment on EVERY video. Write in your own voice so people naturally understand, “This is Troy—the guy in the video.” For example: “Troy here—ask me anything about what we did in this video.”
  • Build an actual relationship. Reply thoughtfully, recognize returning viewers and make people feel heard. We need a strong, healthy community that knows you, not just individual videos that get views.

Shorts to long-form

Use Related Videos to lead people to the full story.

  • Start using the Related Video feature on your Shorts. Connect each relevant Short to the long-form video that continues its story.
  • Give viewers a clear next step. The feature puts a clickable video link below your channel handle in the Shorts player.
  • Make it part of publishing. In YouTube Studio, open the Short, choose a Related Video from your channel and save. Add relevant links to your existing Shorts too.

YouTube requires advanced feature access; the related video must be public or unlisted. YouTube’s related-video guide ↗
