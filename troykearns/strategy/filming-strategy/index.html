Filming Strategy

Mic the main speakers

  • Miking up the main speakers in every video is SUCH a boost to audio quality. Get comfortable even miking up RANDOM people, and say:
  • “I have this YouTube channel, and I think my audience would LOVE listening to what you have to say. Could I put this mic on you and ask you a few questions?”

Keep guests authentic

  • Any more “prepping” of the guest speaker—telling them what “hits” or what they can do to go “viral”—DOES NOT help create viral moments. What goes viral is AUTHENTICITY. Your most viral moments were always authentic, and the audience can smell inauthenticity from miles away.
  • Do NOT prep your guests by telling them to act or speak a certain way. Just ask them great questions. I, the editor, will edit the video to make it seem as if they are taking a super strong stance. I can edit out words like “probably” or “most of the time” to make it seem to the audience that they are 100% certain about their opinion.

Character on camera

  • You, Troy, need to focus on being morally upright, sober-minded, prudent, just, honest, kind, caring and merciful—an all-around amazing guy who gives people the benefit of the doubt. You’ve got to shine the light of Christ in these videos.
  • All of your most VIRAL moments have been when you are standing up for what is right, whether that’s calling someone out on their bullshit lies or affirming someone for doing an amazing job building you an amazing deck. People LOVE IT when you act Christ-like.
  • Let the fruit of the Spirit be on full display: love, joy, peace, forbearance, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control (Galatians 5:22–23, NIV).

Record a quick intro

  • Try to begin new videos with an introduction. I’ll make it short and nice. All I need is: “What’s up, guys? My name’s Troy, and today we’re going to be _______.” Fill in the blank. Just find 20 seconds to introduce yourself to new viewers; that builds trust.
  • Feel free to share a qualifying factor that gives you authority. For example: “I’ve been flipping houses since 2008,” or something similar. Establish yourself as an authority figure, OR establish a GUEST as an authority figure.

Show who’s speaking; collect B-roll

  • Try to always have the camera pointed at the person currently speaking. It’s not a 100% hard-and-fast rule, but please try to point it at the speaker. This checks the box of having something visually interesting on screen at all times.
  • If the camera is pointed at the ground, I’ll be forced to get creative to keep the screen visually interesting. I think you don’t like it when I put things on screen to fill that “empty” space.
  • When no one is speaking, try to point the camera at something around you that is visually interesting. This gives me an abundance of B-roll to help make the video consistently intriguing to the viewer.
