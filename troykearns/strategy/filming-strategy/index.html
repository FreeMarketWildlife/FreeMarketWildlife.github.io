Filming Strategy

Mic the main speakers

  • Mic Troy and the main speakers. For spontaneous encounters, explain the public YouTube use and ask before filming or clipping on a mic. Respect a no.
  • “I have this YouTube channel, and I think my audience would love hearing what you have to say. We’re filming for public YouTube videos—would you be comfortable answering a few questions on camera, and may I clip this mic on you?”
  • Briefly agree on the topic and comfort level. Check mic placement and record a short audio test. Prepare sound and consent, not opinions.

Keep guests authentic

  • Ask strong, open questions. Don’t coach a stance, script reactions or tell guests how to “go viral.”
  • Authentic moments were a strength in the reviewed sample. Use real curiosity to make room for them; authenticity doesn’t guarantee virality.
  • Editor: tighten pauses, repetition and filler while preserving meaning, context and genuine uncertainty. Keep qualifiers such as “probably” when removing them would falsely strengthen someone’s view.

Character on camera

  • Troy, focus on being morally upright, sober-minded, prudent, just, honest, kind, caring and merciful. Give people the benefit of the doubt. Let Christ’s light shine through your conduct.
  • Lean into moments when you stand up for what is right or genuinely recognize someone’s good work. Challenge dishonesty firmly and fairly, with evidence—not unsupported accusations. Credit excellent work, such as someone building a great deck.
  • Let the fruit of the Spirit be visible in how you treat people: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control (Galatians 5:22–23, ESV).

Record a quick intro

  • Find roughly 20 seconds: “What’s up, guys? My name’s Troy, and today we’re going to…” The editor can make it short and clean.
  • Briefly establish Troy’s or a guest’s relevant experience with accurate specifics: “I’ve been flipping houses since [year].” Use only true experience; don’t invent dates or credentials.

Show who’s speaking; collect useful B-roll

  • Normally point the camera at the person speaking. Intentional reactions and B-roll can also help tell the story.
  • Avoid accidental ground shots and empty framing. Get useful coverage instead of forcing the editor to patch gaps with unwanted graphics.
  • When nobody is speaking, film visually interesting surroundings, property details and activity. Give the editor abundant, purposeful B-roll to keep the video consistently engaging.
