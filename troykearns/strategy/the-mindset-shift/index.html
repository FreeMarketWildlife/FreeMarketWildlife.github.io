Influencer Mindset

Trust

  • Being a strong, morally upright, prudent, sober-minded man who treats everybody with love and TRUE integrity has been in EVERY SINGLE ONE OF YOUR VIRAL MOMENTS
  • Build trust with the audience by living a Christ-like life on & off camera, the audience KNOWS whether you're being authentic or not, and the audience LOVES authenticity
  • Focus on keeping your language clean for the sake of NOT pushing away brands for no other reason. Using bad language adds NO Value to your content, and it pushes away audience members and brands with no trade-off. The only possible benefit you can get from it is that it adds to your "authenticity" which is why I suggest you start watching your speech on & off camera (James 3:3-6)

Every project = its own show + playlist

  • Each project gets a playlist, consistent series name/thumbnail identity, clear episode order and a satisfying story per episode.
  • Link the playlist and next released episode; update earlier episodes’ links. Help viewers catch up and return.

Millionaire Mentorship: first sponsor pilot

  • Identify Troy’s own program. Give value, then invite interested viewers to learn more.
  • Track each video → unique campaign link → landing-page visit → completed signup, using one consistent measurement window.
  • Separate free registrations, qualified enquiries and paying customers. Report the offer and attributable results honestly; clicks aren’t signups, and another sponsor’s conversion isn’t guaranteed.

Make Troy recognizable

  • In my Shorts/comments sample, I saw no Related Video links or viewers naming Troy—just references like “the guy in the white hat.” Sample observation only.
  • Introduce Troy without delaying the story; name him in descriptions and personal pinned comments with an episode question.
  • Reply in free time: thoughtful answers or genuine emoji acknowledgments. Prefer meaningful exchanges over repetitive replies.
  • One-month goal: more genuine name recognition. Record a baseline; don’t solicit “Troy” comments to inflate it.

Publish, respond, measure

  • Before publishing: check story, introduction, description, pinned comment, disclosures, Related Video, series identity, project playlist and next-episode route.
  • Reply to comments, don't underestimate how much it means to somebody for you, Troy, to reply personally to comments. Try to find time, even if it's just a few minutes every day, to reply to these comments. They spent the time to write those comments which are helping your channel be massively successful, people are more encouraged to comment when they see you "Hearting" and replying to a ton of comments

Shorts publishing checklist

  • Immediate hook; curiosity loop with satisfying payoff; complete story with purposeful progression.
  • Editorial minimum: 20 seconds, without padding. Build in an irresistible, truthful thumbnail frame; select it where supported.
  • No filler. Rework or hold failed clips. No checklist guarantees winners; judge results after release.

Short → full episode

  • Attach every episode-derived Short to its corresponding long-form video with YouTube Related Video. Check the clickable link; the episode must deliver the Short’s promise.
  • Requires advanced feature access and a same-channel public/unlisted target. Add a natural invitation when useful.

Long-form: promise, then payoff

  • First-30-second target: situation, stakes, possible changes and questions answered. Tease genuine payoffs.
  • Start immediately; use a flexible 15–60-second introduction, without forced montages. Title, thumbnail and opening make one promise; the rest delivers.
  • Early exits can indicate mismatch or confusion—not proof of dislike. Staying doesn’t prove love.
