Influencer Mindset01 — 09
01 / Value firstTroy Kearns · Strategy

Give value before
asking for anything.

Stop making content that feels like an ad.
Make ads worth watching.

Every video owes the viewer education, entertainment, or both. A sponsored segment earns its place by showing a useful solution, demonstrating something interesting, or answering a question the audience cares about.

Be transparent about sponsorships and ownership. Remove the interruption, not the disclosure. Viewers should know when something is promotional and still feel glad they watched.

The test

If someone never clicks or buys, was this still worth their time?

Disclose the material relationship clearly in the video, not just the description. Identify Troy’s own business as his own. Notify YouTube of paid promotion when its rules apply; that tool does not replace clear disclosure.

02 / The case studyTroy Kearns · Strategy

Your business.
Real evidence.

Build the first sponsor-style case study with Millionaire Mentorship.

Make a brief, relevant segment clearly identifying it as your own program. Give value first, then invite interested viewers to learn more.

Show prospective partners the actual offer, viewers, visits and sign-ups. This is stronger evidence than views alone—not a guarantee that every sponsor gets the same result.

The measurement path

Video → tracked link → landing-page visit → completed sign-up

  • Use distinct campaign links per video.
  • Verify completed sign-ups, not just clicks.
  • Use a consistent measurement window.
  • Distinguish free registrations, qualified enquiries and paying customers.

A proposed test, not an active tracking setup or sponsor agreement. Verify the offer and destination before launch.

03 / Related VideoTroy Kearns · Strategy

Give every Short
a next chapter.

Every Short taken from a long-form episode should attach its corresponding full episode with YouTube Related Video.

The Short delivers its own satisfying payoff, then gives viewers a natural reason to continue.

In the Shorts sample I reviewed, I did not see this feature being used. This leaves a useful route unused; missed growth cannot be quantified from the review alone.

Check the connection

  • The correct full episode is selected.
  • The clickable link below the handle is checked.
  • The full episode delivers the Short’s promise.
  • A brief invitation appears when natural.

Setup: advanced feature access is required. The target must be on the same channel and public or unlisted. A description link or pinned comment does not replace Related Video.

04 / Know TroyTroy Kearns · Strategy

Help people
know Troy.

Remember a person, not “that guy in the white hat.”

In the comments I reviewed, I did not see viewers using your name. References were along the lines of that dude or the guy in the white hat.

The goal for next month: Troy appears more often in genuine viewer conversation. Record a baseline from a defined sample first, then compare a similar sample. Don’t ask people to type “Troy” to inflate a metric. We want recognition, not manufactured numbers.

Make the introduction natural

  • In video: introduce yourself without delaying the story.
  • In the description: say who you are and what to expect.
  • In a pinned comment: use your name, a personal observation and an episode question.
  • In free time: reply genuinely. Thoughtful answers are ideal; a genuine emoji acknowledgment still shows someone was seen.

Prioritize meaningful exchanges over repetitive replies. The comment observation describes Phil’s reviewed sample, not a verified whole-channel finding.

Influencer Mindset05 — 09
05 / TrustTroy Kearns · Strategy

Let character
build trust.

Establishing trust with your audience is the foundation of a healthy community.

Viewers should follow because they respect how you act, rather than waiting for you to fail.

Clean language and respectful treatment support broad brand compatibility. They don’t guarantee every sponsor will be a fit.

People should know, like and trust Troy

  • Treat people fairly.
  • Credit expertise and let others teach.
  • Explain the decisions and tradeoffs.
  • Follow through on what you say.
  • Own mistakes without manufactured drama or perfection.
Influencer Mindset06 — 09
06 / ShortsTroy Kearns · Strategy

Every Short must
earn its place.

No filler. No posting just to fill a slot.

If a clip fails the editorial checklist, rework it or hold it. Don’t pad footage to meet a length target; find the story worth telling.

The checklist cannot certify future winners. It can prevent us from publishing clips that haven’t earned a chance. Judge the results after release.

The publishing checklist

  1. Hook: a specific, immediate reason to stop.
  2. Curiosity: an open loop with a satisfying payoff.
  3. Story: purposeful progression through the final moment.
  4. Length: at least 20 seconds under our current editorial standard, with no padding. This is not an algorithm rule.
  5. Thumbnail moment: an irresistible, truthful frame built into the footage, deliberately selected where supported.
  6. Next chapter: the corresponding long-form episode attached.
Influencer Mindset07 — 09
07 / Long-formTroy Kearns · Strategy

Make the promise
impossible to ignore.

Win the first 30 seconds. Then deliver.

The right viewer should think, “I want to see how this turns out.” Establish what is happening, why it matters, what could change and which questions will be answered.

Tease the strongest genuine stakes and moments without giving away every payoff. Start immediately. A flexible 15–60 second introduction can work; don’t force a 60-second montage when a tighter opening is better.

One promise, kept throughout

The title, thumbnail and first 30 seconds make the same promise. The rest fulfills it.

“Unskippable” is the ambition, not a literal guarantee. Early exits can signal a weak, confusing or mismatched setup. They don’t prove those viewers would hate the video—or that everyone who stays loves it.

08 / One monthTroy Kearns · Strategy

Turn the shift
into evidence.

One month. Clear standards. Honest learning.

Before publishing: check the story, introduction, description, pinned comment, Related Video and disclosures.

During the week: Troy replies. Record questions, remembered people and requests for follow-up stories.

At month’s end: review genuine name recognition, references to earlier episodes, returning viewers, retention, format overlap, and pilot visits and completed sign-ups.

Make better decisions

YouTube Studio Viewer (Limited) access would help validate the direction without editing content or seeing revenue data.

This permission can edit Analytics groups and does not provide YouTube API access. Measurement depends on what is available in Studio and the pilot tracking.

More people know Troy, return for his stories and trust his recommendations—not just spikes.
