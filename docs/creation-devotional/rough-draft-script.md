## Introduction — rough spoken draft

[Opening statistic pending: Phil wants the percentage of U.S. Christians who accept an Earth millions or billions of years old, and the percentage who believe dinosaurs did not coexist with humans. No verified national statistic for that exact combined position has been established. Do not substitute an evolution statistic as though it measured these beliefs.]

Many Christians in the United States believe the Earth is millions or billions of years old. Many also believe dinosaurs died out before human beings existed. I want us to stop and think about what we believe about those things—and why.

My purpose for this devotional today is to help you think carefully about a secondary issue: the age of the Earth.

And I want to emphasize that secondary issues matter. Calling something secondary does not mean it is unimportant or that we should never examine it. We can recognize that an issue does not determine whether someone belongs to Christ and still take seriously what Scripture teaches about it.

Some would describe this as an issue of scriptural authority. Here is the question I want us to consider: are we going to take man's word, or God's Word, about how old the Earth is?

Now, Christians who disagree about the Earth's age may both say they believe the authority of Scripture. So I need to show from Scripture why I believe it teaches what I am arguing, rather than simply assume that everyone who disagrees rejects the Bible.

In recent centuries, people have argued that the Bible does not tell us the age of the Earth. Today, I am going to argue that when we read its account of creation and its chronology together, the Bible points to an Earth no older than 10,000 years.

That is the case I want to make. And as we go through this devotional, I want to explain why I believe this issue matters.

## Research notes — checked October 29, 2026

### Exact requested statistic: unresolved
Earth's age, human evolution, and dinosaur–human coexistence are separate survey questions. Do not infer a national Christian percentage for one from a result about another, or combine percentages from separate surveys into a joint percentage. “Christians” in survey reporting refers to the survey's religious identification categories, not a theological assessment.

### Recent related statistic, available as an alternative opening
Pew Research Center's 2023–24 Religious Landscape Study, published February 26, 2025, reports that 61% of U.S. Christians say humans evolved through processes guided or allowed by God or a higher power. This is one response category, not the total percentage accepting evolution. The result does not directly measure Earth's age or beliefs about dinosaurs.

Possible alternate first line, only if Phil wants to broaden the hook:
“In Pew Research Center's 2023–24 study, 61 percent of American Christians said human evolution was guided or allowed by God. That isn't the same question as the age of the Earth, but it shows why we need to examine what Christians believe about origins.”

Source: https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/02/26/religious-and-spiritual-beliefs/

### Older dinosaur-specific result, narrower population
YouGov reported on June 18, 2015 that 22% of Americans describing themselves as born again believed humans and dinosaurs did not coexist; 56% believed they did. These are born-again respondents, not all U.S. Christians. This older result cannot support the claim that most U.S. Christians reject coexistence, and does not measure Earth's age.

Source: https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/12615-jurassic-world

### Research limitation
The searches performed did not establish a recent, nationally representative percentage for all U.S. Christians accepting an old Earth, or for all U.S. Christians rejecting dinosaur–human coexistence. Keep the requested numeric opening pending rather than invent a figure. Old-Earth creationism and acceptance of human evolution must not be treated as interchangeable.
