How to load and launchChoose the winding height with the slider and press Wind counterweight. After loading, hold Aim and release to withdraw the trigger pin. A torque-limited motor physically winds the counterweight. Pause or hiding this tab freezes simulation time. Lift the critter straight up beside the green cradle first. Move across only when the whole critter is above its floor, wait for it to catch up, then release your grip and let it drop in. Pulling straight through the cradle from below hits its solid floor. Contacts and gravity seat it; no radial snap or settling timer. The drag spring has a mass-scaled force limit (shown when capped). Optional tap-to-hold keeps that same force-driven grip until a second tap or Let go. The thin tether connects your actual force target to the real grip point; amber means the force limit is reached. Rotating the view releases the grip and brings the canvas back into view. Unlatch releases the physical arm from the finite frame. The split pouch has a permanent hinge stop against inward collapse; loss of strap support lets its panel fold outward. The meter sets a reachable winding target, not a pose or speed bonus. Motor work and actual height remain simulation outputs. Green and blue sling marks follow the real anchors; dashes mean measured slack. Amber dots mark actual pouch contact; they do not certify full containment. The weight label shows the real rigidly mounted counterweight mass. After physical release the camera centers on the real horizontal center of mass, including backward motion, with a small vertical dead zone. The dotted hint fades before first obstacles. Amber edge arrows warn of approaching fixed branches, wooden deflectors, ground and moving encounters near the edge of view; they estimate nearby passes, not guaranteed collisions. Encounter outlines show their actual circle or capsule contacts. Waxleaf and seed-husk skids have broad slippery backs; pods and fungi rebound more. A ↔ badge on an amber warning marks an approaching skid. These passive contacts can also slow or deflect a poor approach. After launch, arrows provide small arcade nudges; for flying squirrels, right dives and left climbs using lift/drag.

CounterweightGreater mass changes gravitational torque and energy. Material changes the weight’s size and rotational inertia. A hinged weight swings on its own joint; hanger length changes its motion. Fixed weights turn with the beam.
SlingEach piece adds 15 cm of strap and 120 g of pouch reinforcement. Extra length changes swing and release timing.
Wood and shapeTimber mass and arm proportions change balance, leverage and inertia.
FoundationEach brace adds a real wooden cross member and 15 kg of frame mass to resist recoil. Foot friction depends on the equipped feet, not the number of braces. Timber breakage is not simulated.
Sling releaseRotates the physical release guide, changing when the free strap slides off. This is a mounting angle, not the projectile’s launch angle. Our guide is a planar approximation of a release finger.
